# Crisp-drawn OSD banner on macOS 26

Status: approved design, pre-implementation.
Date: 2026-09-02
Issue: #76

## Purpose

Crisp shows an on-screen display for every brightness or volume key press it routes to a display, through the private OSDUIHelper XPC service. On macOS 26 that service draws the pre-Tahoe bezel: a 200 by 200 point square at the bottom centre of the screen with a sixteen-chiclet bar. macOS 26 itself draws a compact glass capsule under the menu bar at the top right of the screen, with the device name and a slider. Red-Pumpkin20 asked on #76 (2026-09-01) for the modern look. MonitorControl gave up on this (their discussions 1799 and 1873: the Tahoe banner is unreachable for third parties, and on 26.0 the helper call landed in the new capsule with an empty bar). BetterDisplay draws its own capsule. Verified on this Mac (26.5.1, Dell U4919DW, 2026-09-02): the helper window belongs to OSDUIHelper at level 2005; the native capsule is a Control Center window at the same level, 352 by 152 points including transparent margins, the visible capsule about 280 by 54 points, 20 points in from the right edge and 12 below the menu bar.

Decision (Didrik, 2026-09-02): on macOS 26 and later Crisp draws its own banner in the Tahoe style. macOS 14 and 15 keep the helper call, whose bezel still matches the system there. No setting, no classic-style option; Crisp stays lightweight.

## Scope

- The triggers do not change. The three key paths in BrightnessKeyService (under-cursor brightness, all-displays and selected-displays brightness, DDC volume and mute) keep calling `BrightnessHUDService.show(level:image:on:)`.
- On macOS 26 and later that call draws a Crisp banner on the target screen. Below 26 it runs the existing OSDUIHelper code unchanged.
- No preference, no new user-facing string. The label is `NSScreen.localizedName`, for volume as well: the native capsule names the audio device, and the display is the audio device in that path.
- Interactive while the pointer is on it, as the native capsule is: it holds itself up, grows a knob that sets the level, and takes a click on a close badge. See Hover below.
- No OSD for brightness changes from other sources (panel sliders, presets, crispctl, auto brightness). Same as today.

## Look

A Liquid Glass capsule, `NSGlassEffectView` regular style, corner radius half the height, 280 by 54 points. Placed on the target screen with its right edge 20 points in from the screen's right edge and its top 12 points below the visible frame's top, so it sits under the menu bar, or 12 points below the screen edge when the menu bar is hidden.

Inside, top to bottom: the display name in a small secondary label, then a level track with a symbol at each end. Brightness: `sun.min.fill` and `sun.max.fill`. Volume: `speaker.fill` and `speaker.wave.3.fill`. Mute: `speaker.slash.fill` on the left, an empty track, no trailing symbol. The track is a thin capsule in the secondary label colour with a primary-colour fill sized by the level. Text and symbols use the primary and secondary label colours so the glass adapts to light and dark on its own. Exact sizes, weights and paddings are tuned live against a screenshot of the native capsule on the same screen.

Window level 2005, the value both OSDUIHelper and Control Center use on 26.5.1, so the banner sits where the system's own does. Collection behaviour joins all Spaces and shows over full-screen apps, like the menu panel.

As built (2026-09-03, measured against the native volume HUD on the Dell U4919DW, both over the same backdrop so the numbers compare): 292 by 64 points, top 10 points below the menu bar, corner radius 20 with a continuous curve, centred on Crisp's menu bar item (see Placement below) or, with no item to measure, 10 points in from the right edge. Measure the native capsule at rest, a second after the key press: it settles down from above through the first half second, and a frame caught during that settle reads 2 points narrower, 1 point shorter and rounder than the capsule the eye sees, which is where the earlier 290 by 63 at radius 26 came from. The capsule is not a glass view. NSGlassEffectView was tried first, on the public clear and regular styles and on all 24 private materials, and none of them can hold the HUD's backdrop: the materials that blur wash the backdrop out well past the HUD, and the ones that do not leave it sharp. A Core Image filter over the glass cannot correct this either: the window server composites the backdrop, not this process, so a filter on a layer above it changes nothing, whatever the radius. So the banner draws its own backdrop: a CABackdropLayer sampling at half resolution with a CAFilter gaussianBlur at radius 3.2 over it, inside a layer masked to the capsule shape. Both numbers are fitted against the HUD's own transfer curve, measured on a backdrop of dark bar groups of eight widths under both capsules: what share of a group's 204-level contrast survives at 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 and 12 points wide. The HUD passes 45, 61, 71, 76, 93 and 119 from 2 to 12 points, and the banner 28, 40, 47, 73, 96 and 122. The wide end is the end the eye reads, because a toolbar icon or a word behind the capsule lives there, and it is the end a single radius sets: the earlier radius, fitted on fine structure alone, passed 108, 118 and 126 at 6, 8 and 12 points, which is why icons stayed legible through the banner and not through the HUD. What is left on this axis is the narrow end, where the banner passes about two thirds of what the HUD does. No one gaussian holds both ends, because the HUD's kernel has a narrower core and a wider skirt than a gaussian has.

The same layer bends its backdrop into the edge, the way an edge of glass does, which is what the HUD does and a plain masked capsule does not: behind the HUD a straight line curves along the inside of the edge, behind a hard-cut capsule it runs straight off. The bend is the system's own glass filter, put on our layer and pointed at a distance-field layer for the shape, which is why it draws nothing on a bare backdrop layer. Its face is left off, since the tone is carried in the same chain, and its own blur is set to zero, since its default is heavy enough to take the fine structure to a third of what the HUD leaves. It was first fitted as displacement rather than by eye: a stripe backdrop of one period behind both capsules, and the phase of that pattern read column by column in from the edge. The HUD pulls its backdrop 4.8 points sideways at the strongest point, 8 points in from the edge, holds a weaker shoulder at 15 points and lets go 26 points in, and -110 over a height of 20 draws 4.4 points at the same place, with the same shoulder and the same release. That measure gets the shape of the bend but not its strength, because it says where the backdrop goes and not how dark it lands, and the tone is what the eye reads: at -110 the banner carried a dark band along the bottom edge over a bright window that the HUD does not have. So the amount is fitted on tone instead, over a backdrop of bands, as how far each column in from the edge sits from the flat tone in the middle of the capsule. The HUD reads 33, 27, 22, 20, 19, 18, 13, 11 and 11 levels off at 2 to 10 points in; -110 reads 35, 30, 27, 22, 20, 19, 17, 14 and 12, a quarter strong the whole way in, and -80 reads 32, 27, 21, 21, 20, 17, 14, 12 and 11, within a level of the HUD at every column. A probe capsule used for this has to sit exactly where the banner sits: an early sweep put its capsule a point further in, read every column one across, and made -90 look like the answer. The height is not a dial: 16 bends nothing at all and 30 folds the backdrop over itself. Well under the fitted amount there is no bend the eye can find at all, which is what the first attempt here (-12 over 8, fitted by eye at 8x) drew. Every class and key on that path is private, so each is asked for by name and any one missing leaves the banner with a blurred backdrop and no bend.

The tone and the colour ride in the same chain, after the blur and before the bend: multiply by 0.657, add 31, saturate by 1.92. The first two are the native tone line, which is linear in the backdrop (out = 0.657 in + 31), and on backdrops of black, mid grey and 233 the banner reads 31, 130 and 184 to the native's 31, 129 and 184. The third is what a grey alone cannot do. Measured on four saturated backdrops the HUD lands on that same brightness line to a tenth of a level and multiplies what is left of the colour by 1.26 every time, where a grey at alpha 0.343 can only keep 0.657 of it, so a coloured window behind the banner stayed visibly duller than behind the HUD; saturating by 1.26 / 0.657 after the line puts it back, and over a strong green the HUD reads 17, 168, 55 and the banner 18, 168, 55. The order matters, because saturating the backdrop before the line drives a strong colour past black in one channel and clips it. Any of those filters missing leaves the banner one flat grey layer instead, white 0.355 at alpha 0.343, which draws the same line without the colour. The content sits above all of it, so the label, glyphs and track keep their own tone, and it is forced to the dark appearance on the hosting view only: the native capsule is dark with white text in either appearance, and reads the same in both, and so does this one. Label 12.25 point regular, 16 points in, on a line box held at 16 points so the track and the glyphs keep their own rows, with its baseline 22.75 points below the capsule top; glyphs 13 point at the same inset; the track 4 points thick, its centre 40.5 points below the top and one point above the glyph centre line, 4 points from each glyph, the unfilled part 7 percent white, with the native's 17 tick dots (2 points, 11 percent white, 6 points below the track centre, 4.5 points in from each end). The label, the glyphs and the track fills are all explicit white, never the primary label colour, which is white at 85 percent in the dark appearance and measures 243 over the same body where the HUD's label measures 251, reading thinner and duller. The dark appearance still goes on the hosting view, for anything semantic left in the content. The label size is measured, not chosen: the same string drawn at 13, 12.5 and 12 points beside the HUD's own label, and each one matched to it by sliding one ink profile over the other at every scale, puts the HUD at 12.33, 12.22 and 12.18 points, and the ascender heights (18.5 pixels at 2x against 19.7, 18.9 and 18.1) agree. The 13 points the banner used before is what read as slightly off next to the HUD without being nameable. Compare like with like here: an ink or stroke width read off two different strings measures the strings, and an AppKit probe measures its own tracking, since NSAttributedString does not apply the optical tracking a SwiftUI Text does. On the same screen the label now sits on the HUD's rows, ascender top to baseline, and the track sits on the same four rows with the same left inset and right end. The native capsule carries a bevel, a rim one point wide along its edge, which the banner draws as a layer border of white at alpha 0.36 on a view over the content, so it follows the corner radius and the entry and exit resize. It reads 112 over a body of 31 on black and 175 over 130 on mid grey to the native's 105 over 31 and 176 over 129. Two differences are left. At 2 and 3 points of backdrop detail the banner passes about two thirds of what the HUD does, since a gaussian cannot follow the HUD's kernel at both ends. And over a near-white backdrop the native's rim fades into the backdrop while the banner's stays a faint line, 210 to the native's 233. Entry, hold and exit are fitted to the system HUD frame by frame, against the tone the screen actually shows: the native capsule takes about half a second to darken along a curve with a slow start while it grows in from 11 points narrower and 2 points shorter on each side and settles down 5.5 points over 0.35 seconds, eased out. It holds 1 second after the last press, then lifts back, shrinks a little further than it grew from (14 by 3 points a side) over 0.45 seconds along a cubic bezier with control points (0.2, 0.4) and (0.3, 1), and fades on its own group: the native exit falls fast and tails off, and a plain ease-out ends 0.1 seconds too early. The fade in runs 0.55 seconds on a bezier with control points (0.4, 0.05) and (0.2, 0.9), the fade out 0.54 seconds on (0.2, 0.65) and (0.35, 1). Both were re-measured after the backdrop layer replaced the glass and both still hold: through the entry the banner tracks the native within a frame or two at every sample, and through the exit the two fall together to a twentieth, where the native keeps a small tail about 80 milliseconds longer. A press during the entry lets it finish. A press during the exit stops the exit where it is and comes back up from there: a second animation on a window property does not replace the one in flight, the two drive the window together and the exit wins, so the banner used to blink out and come back before the press took hold.

The dots mean something, which they did not before. macOS moves brightness and volume between sixteen fixed stops, 6.25 apart, and the HUD's seventeen dots mark them: three steps up its fill ends on the third dot, eleven steps up on the eleventh, to a tenth of a pixel at 2x, and only at the top of the range does it run past the last dot to the end of the track. Two things kept the banner off them. Its keys added or took away a sixteenth of the range from wherever the value happened to be, so a display found at 79 percent went to 85.25, 91.5 and 97.75 and never touched a stop; `Models/BrightnessKeySteps.swift` now returns the next stop above or below instead, which is what the system's own keys do, and the caller still clamps it to the display's maximum so Extra Brightness carries the grid on past 100. And the track drew its fill as a plain fraction of its width with the dots 6 points in, where the HUD insets its dots 4.5 points and ends the fill on the dot for the level. With both fixed, the fill lands on the dot at every step. Two cases are left where it cannot: with Extra Brightness on the track spans 0 to the display's maximum, so a step is half a dot, and on a display whose own brightness is not on the grid, the first press moves it there rather than a whole step.

## Placement

The banner hangs under Crisp's own menu bar item, and lights it while it holds, which is what the system does with its own HUDs. Measured on 26.5.1 with two screens: the brightness HUD's capsule spans 1072 to 1364 points on a 1512 point screen, centre 1218, and the Display control's item window is 1200 to 1237, centre 1218.5. The volume HUD looks like a corner banner, right edge 10 points in, and is not: the Sound control sits 123 points from the screen edge, so centring the capsule on it would put its right edge a point past the screen, and 10 points in is where it lands when it cannot centre. So the rule is centre on the owning item, and no closer than 10 points to either side edge, which reproduces both HUDs where they sit.

The item is lit the way the system lights the Sound control while its own HUD is up: the same standard button highlight, a rounded background 24 points tall and about four points wider than the item, which reads as the same overlay on the same wallpaper (measured on the menu bar: the system lifts the item by 16, 17 and 19 levels of red, green and blue, and Crisp's by 12, 16 and 20). It goes out as the banner starts to leave, not when it has gone: the system's own item goes dark between 0.85 and 0.95 seconds after the press, with its capsule still half there, and Crisp's now goes dark in the same bracket. An open Crisp panel holds the same highlight, so the two are read together and neither turns the other off.

Crisp's item lives on one screen at a time, and the menu bar draws the same items on every screen, so on a screen the item is not on the banner mirrors its offset from the right edge; AppDelegate.positionPanel already places the menu panel that way. Measured in all-displays mode with two screens up at once, each banner's centre lands within two points of its own screen's copy of the icon. A menu bar with no room for the item leaves that item's window away from the bar, which the anchor drops, and the banner keeps the top right corner it had before. The lit item is the real one, so it lights on the screen the item is on, which is not always the screen the banner is on.

## Hover

The capsule takes the pointer the way the system's does. The pointer resting on it holds it up, grows a knob on the track and puts a close badge on the top left corner; dragging the knob sets that display's level as it moves, and the badge sends the banner away. The pointer leaving starts the 1 second hold again.

The knob is 18 by 14 points, white at 0.85, and a capsule rather than a circle: Crisp's own slider draws a capsule 20 by 16 at the small control size, and the native knob measures the same way, 8 points wide half a point in from its top edge where a circle of that height would be 7. White at 0.85 and not white is what fits it over five backdrops, where the native knob reads 222 over black and 247 over white and a flat colour reads the same on both. Its centre travels the track inset by half its width, which is not the tick grid the resting fill follows: a click a quarter of the way along the native track set its volume to 23, not 25, because what the pointer moves is the knob's travel. While the knob is out the fill stops at its leading edge instead of running under it, so the knob sits on the body alone and its tone lands where the native's does.

The close badge is a disc 18 points across, its centre 6.5 points in from the capsule's top left corner, so it hangs 2.5 points over it. It does not sit on the capsule at all: swept over six flat backdrops the native disc reads 178, 185, 199, 224, 238 and 249 where the desktop reads 56, 79, 116, 169, 211 and 255, a tone line of its own laid over the desktop (0.373 of it plus 157), and no white at any alpha can draw that (0.65 lands on the middle, 0.90 on the top, so over a light desktop a flat badge reads as a grey disc where the native is nearly clear). So the badge is AppKit, on the same private backdrop layer as the capsule, with its own tone filters and a heavier blur: over a checkerboard the native badge leaves 3 levels of the backdrop's 59 where the capsule leaves 15. The two tone numbers are fitted rather than the measured line, because the filters do not realise what they are asked for. The glyph over it is a 9 point bold `xmark` in black at half: the native's ink is 90, 93, 100, 112, 119 and 125 over those same six discs, the same half of the disc every time, so it is not a flat grey. Measure any of this on a settled badge, a second after the pointer lands: the badge's own 0.29 second fade moves the disc by up to 14 levels, which is what made an earlier reading of the native line curve where it is straight.

Under the disc is a shadow, which is what makes the badge an object over a light desktop rather than a disc that disappears into it. A layer shadow cannot draw it, since the native reaches more than 20 points past the disc and a CALayer shadow at any radius has faded by then, so it is a radial gradient with its stops read off the native over white, where the white drops 33 levels at the disc's edge, 20 seven points out, 10 eleven points out and is still 3 down 22 points out. It is masked to the outside of the disc: the disc samples the desktop and lets anything under it through, which took ten levels off its own tone.

The badge and its shadow need room, so the window is 30 points bigger than the capsule on every side and the capsule, its content, the bevel and the hover view all sit inside that margin. They resize with the window, so their margins survive the entry grow and the exit shrink.

Not matched: over a backdrop darker than about level 35 the native badge turns into a dark disc with a light cross, which ours does not. The flip is a state the system picks from the backdrop it samples, not a tone curve (over a checkerboard the native disc stays flat, and public glass does not flip at all: its disc tracks the backdrop and its ink stays dark at every tone). Reading the backdrop ourselves would need Screen Recording, so ours stays light.

The badge fades in, and it is the only part that does anything on the way in. It comes up on its opacity alone, 0.29 seconds in on an ease-out only a little faster than a straight line and 0.35 out running straight, both fitted to a 75 frames a second recording of the pointer landing on the native HUD; it does not grow and it does not move. The knob and its fill are not animated at all, they are there the frame the pointer is, and their edges sit in the same columns at every step of the badge's fade. Measure this with the banner settled, a good half second after the press: a pointer that lands while the banner is still coming up, or already leaving, reads the banner's own 0.55 second fade instead of the badge's.

Two things about the pointer on a window like this one. SwiftUI's `.onHover` wants a key window and this panel never becomes key, so the tracking is an `NSTrackingArea` in a plain view over the hosting view, with `hitTest` returning nil so the clicks still reach the content. And `ignoresMouseEvents` is off only while the pointer is on the capsule, because a window takes every click inside it whatever its views hand back, and the window is wider than the capsule: a view that returns nil stops the view below it from seeing the click, not the window below the window, so the margin would swallow clicks on the menu bar over the banner. The tracking area fires whether the window takes clicks or not, which is what makes that safe, and it is also why a hover arriving at a banner already at alpha 0 is refused outright: the window stays where it was and the pointer crossing that corner would otherwise bring back a banner nobody asked for.

## Structure

`Services/OSDBannerService.swift`, `@available(macOS 26.0, *)`, `@MainActor`, a shared singleton:

- `panels: [CGDirectDisplayID: OSDBannerPanel]`, one per screen, created on first use for that screen and never ordered out afterwards. Taking a glass surface off screen replays the materialize bloom on the next order-in (the menu panel note in AppDelegate), so hidden means alpha 0. Every show from hidden plays the entry animation (see As built), which also masks the one-time bloom.
- `show(level:image:on:)`: prune panels whose display is no longer in `NSScreen.screens`, get or create the panel for the screen, update the model, then `reveal(at:)` with the frame recomputed from the screen (resolution changes covered): the entry animation when hidden or fading, a plain move when visible, and the hide timer restarted either way.
- Hide timer: 1 second after the last show, then the 0.45 second shrink with the fade to alpha 0 beside it.
- Key repeat only touches the model and the timer. No allocation per press.
- The panel: borderless, non-activating, `ignoresMouseEvents` while hidden (see Hover), `isOpaque` false, clear background, no AppKit window shadow (the glass edge carries the shape; the edge profile matched the native capsule to within two pixels without one), `animationBehavior` none, `hidesOnDeactivate` false, level 2005, collection behaviour transient, ignores cycle, can join all Spaces, full-screen auxiliary.
- Content: an `NSGlassEffectView` filling the panel, its `contentView` an `NSHostingView` of the banner view.

`Views/OSDBannerView.swift`: SwiftUI, driven by an observable model with title, kind (brightness, volume, mute) and level in 0 to 1 (the service divides the call site's percentage by 100). About 40 lines.

`BrightnessHUDService.show(level:image:on:)`: `if #available(macOS 26.0, *)` forward to the banner service and return, otherwise the existing XPC code. The `OSDImage` enum stays as the shared vocabulary of the call sites.

## Edge cases

- All-displays mode adjusts several screens per press and shows one banner per screen at the same time. Per-screen panels cover it.
- The built-in display shows the Crisp banner in the all-displays and selected modes, since Crisp consumes the key there and macOS draws nothing. In under-cursor mode with the pointer on the built-in, Crisp passes the key through and macOS draws its own capsule; the two never overlap.
- Extra Brightness: the call sites already pass the level as a percentage of the display's extended maximum, so the track shows the position within the extended range, as the chiclets did.
- Screen goes away while a banner is parked: the panel stays at alpha 0 until the next show prunes it.
- A screen without a matching NSScreen never reaches the service; the call sites guard that already.
- The Crisp panel is open. No banner then: the panel shows the same level on a slider a few points away, and the two moving together is noise. `BrightnessHUDService.suppressed` is set while the panel is up, so this holds on 14 and 15 as well.
- A still screen. A layer that samples what is behind it needs the screen composited, and WindowServer stops compositing a screen with nothing changing on it, which leaves the sample empty: the tone line turns an empty sample into level 31 and the capsule reads black until something on screen moves. Holding brightness up at 100 percent is that case, because the level does not move either, so nothing in the banner redraws; on a display driven by gamma the press writes nothing at all, and the decrease key only fixes it because a new gamma table refreshes the whole screen. Every screen capture of it looks correct, since a capture forces a composite, which is why this was found by reasoning and confirmed on Didrik's screen rather than measured. The backdrop layer therefore carries `windowServerAware`, which the system's own glass sets on its backdrop layer, and an animation the eye cannot see (a thousandth of the layer's opacity, taken off as the banner goes) that keeps the layer rendering while the banner is up. EDROverlayManager keeps its overlay alive against the same promotion, by re-presenting at 5 fps. Neither change moves the look: the transfer curve, the tone on three backdrops and the edge displacement all measure the same afterwards.

## Compatibility

- `NSGlassEffectView` exists in the macOS 26 SDK only, and CI builds on that SDK with a macOS 14 deployment target. The service file is gated with `@available(macOS 26.0, *)`, the call with `#available`.
- Both build modes must stay green: `swiftc -swift-version 5` in `make dev` and Swift 6 in Xcode with minimal strict concurrency. Everything in the service is main-actor bound.
- Strict SwiftLint is part of `make check`.

## Testing and verification

No unit test: the only pure logic is the frame arithmetic, two lines. Live verification with `make dev`:

- Brightness keys with the pointer on the Dell U4919DW: banner at the top right of the Dell, level tracks the key steps, fades 1 second after the last press, repeats while the key repeats.
- Volume and mute keys with a DDC-volume display as the audio output, if one is at hand; otherwise the volume and mute variants are checked with a temporary dev-only call in the brightness path, removed before the PR.
- All-displays mode with the lid open: a banner on each screen at once, including the built-in.
- A screenshot of the Crisp banner next to the native capsule on the same screen, for the look.
- Hover: the banner holds while the pointer is on it, the knob and badge fade in, a click on the track sets that display's brightness or volume, the badge sends it away, and the pointer crossing the corner where a banner used to be brings nothing back.
- Lint and compile green; `make test` runs in CI (no Xcode on this Mac).
